The McDonnell Aircraft Corporation Project Mercury Familiarization Manual (SEDR 104) blueprints establish the definitive engineering baseline for the spacecraft's semi-monocoque titanium pressure vessel and structural architecture. [1, 2]

When applying these historical blueprints to your Friendship 8 design, the complete engineering descriptions across the primary structural categories break down into specific fabrication layers, loads, and mating constraints.

* * * * *

1\. The Cabin Pressure Shell & Inner Skin Structure
---------------------------------------------------

The primary structural load-bearing and airtight boundary is documented in SEDR 104 as a double-walled semi-monocoque titanium assembly. [2, 3]

-   Material & Thickness: The inner capsule wall consists of two layers of 0.010-inch sheets of commercially pure titanium (AMS 4901), forming the main pressure cone. These dual skins are seam-welded together to share stress loads and safeguard against microscopic material defects or pinholes.
-   Stiffening Ribs: To prevent structural oil-ganning or buckling under compressed atmospheric loads, the thin titanium skin is welded directly to a series of longitudinal hat-section stringers and circumferential Z-framing, also formed from titanium.
-   The Pressure Bulkheads:

    -   Large Pressure Bulkhead (Aft): A flat, circular titanium disk stiffened with radial beams located directly behind the custom-molded [Support Couch](https://ia600600.us.archive.org/18/items/nasa_techdoc_19790077942/19790077942.pdf). It bears the massive compressive forces from booster acceleration and deceleration.
    -   Small Pressure Bulkhead (Forward): Closes the upper cone section. It provides the penetrations for the Cabin Air Inlet and Outflow Valves to manage post-landing ventilation. [4, 5]

2\. Thermal Shielding & Outer Shingle Shell
-------------------------------------------

The inner titanium pressure vessel does not face the vacuum of space or reentry friction directly. The blueprints detail an outer protective layer separated by an insulating dead-air gap. [2, 3]

-   Heat Shingles: The conical flanks of the capsule are clad in overlapping René 41 nickel-alloy shingles (0.016 to 0.022 inches thick). These shingles expand and distort freely under extreme thermal loads (up to 1,000°F+ during aerodynamic friction) without transferring strain or buckling forces to your underlying seam-welded titanium pressure wall. [3]
-   Aft Heat Shield Mating Ring: The large aft bulkhead terminates in a robust structural ring that mechanically mates via a circumferential V-band clamp to the fiberglass-reinforced ablative heat shield. This shield absorbs the bulk of reentry heating and conceals the pneumatic landing impact cushion. [3]

3\. Structural Mating Boundaries for Redstone Integration
---------------------------------------------------------

For a "drop-in" alignment on a Mercury-Redstone missile adapter, your fabrication must match McDonnell's precise external interface zones:

-   The Base Ring Flange: Located at the widest diameter of the aft section (1.88 meters/74.5 inches). This machined ring must seat flush onto the Capsule Adapter Ring that replaces the standard Redstone weapon nose cone. It is locked together by an explosive marmon clamp tape network. [3, 6]
-   The Retrograde Rocketry Bridge: Crucial for structural layout, the retro-pack attachment points cross directly over the center of the aft heat shield via tension straps anchored into the main titanium framing loops. This ensures that the forward thrust of the posigrade/retrograde firing vector pushes straight through the capsule's core dynamic axis without tweaking the thin-gauge skin.
