1\. Rolling and Seaming the Titanium Can
----------------------------------------

Replicating the iconic 20-degree conical shape using a double-seam and weld approach requires a precise progression of mechanical forming:

-   Stamping and Flanging: The inner skin sections are stamped into their curved conical segments with precise, consistent extended margins. Using automated forming rollers, these margins are bent 90 degrees outward to create the mating flanges.
-   The Double Seam Interlock: Once the stamped panels are fixtured together on the frame, a heavy-duty motorized seaming wheel tracks along the joint. The first operation curls the wider flange over the shorter one; the second operation flattens the five layers tightly against each other, mechanically interlocking the titanium sheets.
-   The Fusion Weld: To complete the "welded can" philosophy, an automated fiber laser or specialized micro-TIG welding torch tracks perfectly along the compressed crown of the double seam. This melts the outer folds into a single solid titanium bead, creating a absolute hermetic seal that completely eliminates micro-leak paths without altering the interior volume.

* * * * *

2\. Modern Gasket & Seal Integration (Zero Asbestos)
----------------------------------------------------

The original capsules relied heavily on silicone rubber seals backed by asbestos-impregnated thermal barriers around the main hatch, window frames, and antenna canister.

To adapt AC Delco-style high-performance automotive gasket philosophies into an aerospace pressure boundary:

-   Molded Fluorosilicone / Viton: Utilize custom-molded, high-density fluorosilicone or Viton-elastomer gaskets designed similarly to high-pressure automotive engine seals. These materials feature excellent compression-set resistance and remain highly flexible at cryogenic orbital temperatures while handling extreme radiant heat.
-   Dual-Lip Compression Geometry: Design the gasket profiles with an interlocking dual-lip arrangement. The primary lip handles internal pressure retention (holding the cabin atmosphere), while the secondary lip acts as a backup environmental seal to block moisture and debris during recovery.

3\. Hybrid Heat Shield: Silica Ceramics & Carbon-Carbon RCC
-----------------------------------------------------------

Because your pressure vessel is fully welded and lacks the loose, overlapping "shingle" gaps of the original craft to expand and contract, managing thermal expansion across the skin is critical. Your proposed hybrid heat shield handles this perfectly:

```
[ Inner Titanium Pressure Hull ]
               │
               ▼
[ Flexible Silica Ceramic Blanket ] (Insulates & absorbs structural expansion)
               │
               ▼
[ Rigid Carbon-Carbon RCC Shell ]   (Handles peak aero-thermic friction)

```

-   Flexible Silica Ceramic Blankets: Lining the conical afterbody with high-purity flexible silica ceramic blankets (similar to modern aerospace thermal protection blankets) offers incredible advantages. It forms a continuous insulation barrier with zero gaps, and its woven flexibility absorbs the microscopic structural expansion of the titanium hull as it cycles between daylight and shadow in space.
-   Carbon-Carbon RCC (Reinforced Carbon-Carbon): Replicating the nose cap, recovery canister tips, and the high-stagnation points of the aft bulkhead using rigid Carbon-Carbon RCC provides elite protection where thermal loads peak. RCC maintains its structural strength well past 3,000°F (1,650°C), shielding the most vulnerable radii of the *Friendship* shape during atmospheric interface.
